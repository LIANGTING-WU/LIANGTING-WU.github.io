abstract: >
  With the rapid pace of industrialization, air pollution has become a serious global environmental concern. Efficient gas sensing plays a crucial role in environmental monitoring and health protection. Transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs) have emerged as promising candidates for gas sensor materials due to their high surface area and tunable electronic properties. In this study, we employed a combined non-equilibrium Green’s function (NEGF) and density functional theory (DFT) approach to investigate the gas sensing properties of monolayer molybdenum diselenide (MoSe2). We explored its interactions and electronic response to eight industrially relevant gas molecules (SO2, SO3, H2O, O2, NO, NO2, CO, and CO2). Among these gases, SO3 exhibited the strongest interaction with the MoSe2 monolayer (−0.61 eV) and the highest charge transfer (0.285 |e|). NO2 and SO2 showed moderate adsorption, whereas CO, CO2, O2, and H2O exhibited weaker interactions, suggesting high selectivity for SO3. Electronic transport properties were evaluated along both armchair and zigzag crystallographic directions, revealing enhanced resistance under gas adsorption in the zigzag configuration, particularly for SO3. At a low bias voltage of 0.05 V, the zigzag orientation achieved a maximum sensitivity of 164.4 %, while the armchair direction reached 31.4 % at 0.57 V. Estimated recovery times for the SO3@MoSe2 system indicated rapid material recovery (τ = 18.53 s), supporting real-time sensing capabilities. Overall, this work highlights the impact of crystallographic orientation on toxic gas sensing by monolayer MoSe2, serving as a guide for designing highly sensitive, selective, and energy-efficient gas sensors for environmental monitoring.
