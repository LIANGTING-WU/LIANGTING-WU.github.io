abstract: >
  Metal-ion batteries hold significant potential as high-energy-density storage devices, yet investigating their microscopic structures to understand material performance remains a great challenge. High-entropy engineering further advances battery materials, such as solid electrolytes and cathodes for Li- and Na-based batteries, where the complexity of atomic arrangements is beyond the capabilities of quantum-mechanics-based modeling methods. Here, we develop a swap Monte Carlo simulation along multiple dimensions (MCMD) algorithm combined with machine-learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) to improve configuration prediction in complex metal-ion battery materials. Compared with previously reported approaches, MCMD dynamically and randomly explores coupled degrees of freedom. Our results show that the new algorithm outperforms traditional SMC. We also propose an automated strategy to sample high-energy regions of the PES; MLIPs fine-tuned using logarithmic energy-space sampling exhibit better generalization than models fine-tuned using a minimum-energy sampling method, particularly for high-energy configurations. These findings highlight the importance of multi-dimensional configuration searching for overcoming limitations of traditional methods and improving energy prediction performance in complex systems. Overall, combining MCMD with MLIPs enhances modeling of metal-ion batteries, offering deeper insights into material behavior while reducing computational cost and enabling more efficient simulations of next-generation battery materials.
