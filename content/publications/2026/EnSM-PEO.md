abstract: >
  Solid polymer electrolytes (SPEs) offer excellent electrode compatibility for solid-state batteries. Here, we develop a novel interpenetrating polymer network electrolyte (IPNE) based on polyethylene oxide (PEO) and polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF), incorporating lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI) salt and synthesized using high-polarity dimethylacetamide (DMAc) to enhance Li+ transport. To achieve high ionic conductivity and interfacial stability, CuF2 and LiNO3 additives are introduced, forming CL-IPNE membranes. These additives react with lithium to generate a robust solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) enriched with LiF and Li3N, ensuring strong adhesion and uniform Li deposition. CuF2 reduction produces Cu0 nanoparticles that spatially separate SEI domains and create additional Li+ pathways, while Cu2+ in the bulk weakens Li+–polymer and Li+–solvent interactions, promoting mobility. Consequently, CL-IPNE exhibits an ionic conductivity of 1.3 mS cm−1 at 30 °C, and Li|CL-IPNE|LiFePO4 cells retain 87% capacity after 400 cycles of charge-discharge at 1.5 mA cm−2. The free-standing membranes are compatible with roll-to-roll processing, enabling scalable, cost-effective manufacturing. This work highlights the synergistic roles of CuF2 and LiNO3 in advancing SPE performance for high-energy solid-state batteries.
